# The story of Ipê

## Why Ipê

In Brasília, in the middle of the dry season, when the city turns brown and the air gets dry, the ipê trees bloom. Yellow, purple, white — and suddenly the landscape changes. I like the idea of a program with that name: something born in the middle of the arid daily work that makes the routine a little lighter.

And there is a coincidence I only noticed later: *ipê* is in my name. Fil**ipe**.

## How it was born

I am a radiologist dedicated to breast imaging and a technology enthusiast. Like many radiologists, I moved to macOS because of OsiriX and Horos, for years the best workstation within reach of those who report studies. But I always wanted a complete and robust program that would also run on Windows and Linux, the systems used by most of the services where we work.

For a long time that wish stayed an idea: I knew what I wanted on the screen, but I could not turn it into a program by myself. Artificial intelligence changed that. Today I describe a need from practice — "in breast MRI, I want the kinetic map"; "in an empty viewport, let me choose the series" — and **Claude**, from Anthropic, works like a senior programmer: it writes the code, tests it and explains what it did. The clinical decisions, what goes in and how it should work remain mine, as does the checking on real studies.

## On the shoulders of giants

Ipê only exists because many people shared their work as open source: libraries for the interface (Qt/PySide6), for DICOM (pydicom, pynetdicom, GDCM, pylibjpeg), for computation (NumPy) and for artificial intelligence, such as **TotalSegmentator**, plus many others listed in *Credits and licenses*. That is why Ipê is open too (GPL-3.0): so that others can use, study, improve and give back.

## What it is not

Ipê is a research and teaching tool. **It is not a registered medical device** and is not intended for diagnostic use.

— Filipe Ramos Barra
